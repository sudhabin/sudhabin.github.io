Impedance Matching Guide & RF Engineering Notes RF PRINCIPLES

⚡ L-Networks: 2-Element Minimum Loss

L-networks achieve mathematically exact matching with minimal component count (2 elements), ensuring the lowest insertion loss. The loaded Q is fixed by $Q = \sqrt{R_{hi}/R_{lo} - 1}$. Low-pass forms suppress transmitter harmonics, while high-pass forms provide DC blocking.

📐 Pi (π) & T-Networks: Bandwidth Control

Pi and T networks introduce an independent virtual intermediate resistance ($R_v$), allowing the designer to specify loaded Q independently of impedance ratio. Pi-networks are favored in RF power amplifiers because shunt capacitors absorb parasitic collector/drain and PCB pad capacitance.

🎯 Real-Time Smith Chart Trajectory

A series reactance moves the impedance point along constant-resistance circles (clockwise for L, counter-clockwise for C). A shunt susceptance moves the admittance point along constant-conductance circles (clockwise for C, counter-clockwise for L), steering the load to the center $50\,\Omega$ match.

📡 Distributed Microstrip Stub Matching

At high microwave frequencies, lumped inductors and capacitors suffer from self-resonance. Single-stub microstrip matching uses an electrical transmission line of length $d$ to transform admittance to $Y_0 + jB$, then cancels $jB$ with an open or shorted shunt stub of length $l$.
