⚡ T-Pad vs Pi-Pad (π): When to Use Which?

Low Attenuations (1 dB – 6 dB): T-pads often feature smaller series resistor values and manageable shunt values, minimizing series parasitic inductances. For a 3 dB 50Ω pad: T uses 8.55Ω series and 142Ω shunt; Pi uses 292Ω shunts and 17.6Ω series.

High Attenuations (10 dB – 30 dB): Pi-pads are standard in planar microstrip circuits because the series resistor value is high (easier to prevent unwanted capacitive RF coupling) while shunts remain close to 50Ω–60Ω.

📡 RF & Microwave Parasitic Considerations

  • Resistor Choice: Use 0603 or 0805 thin-film SMD chip resistors for VHF/UHF/Microwave (>100 MHz). Avoid wirewound resistors due to massive inductance.
  • Lead & Via Inductance: Ground vias have ~0.5–1.0 nH inductance each. In Pi and T shunts, use multiple parallel ground vias directly to the microstrip ground plane.
  • Parallel Capacitance: High-value series resistors have ~0.05–0.1 pF parallel end-cap capacitance, causing high-frequency attenuation roll-off.

🔥 Power Dissipation & Resistor Wattage

In passive attenuators, the input resistors absorb the vast majority of the RF power. Always apply a 2× safety derating factor:

  • If dissipated power is 160 mW, choose a 0.5 W or 1 W rated resistor.
  • In RF transmitters, sudden reflections or VSWR mismatch can double the voltage across shunt components.
