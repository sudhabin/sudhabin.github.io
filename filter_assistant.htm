Filter Design Theory & RF Engineering Notes PRINCIPLES

⚡ Butterworth: Maximally Flat Passband

Butterworth filters provide mathematically maximally flat response ($d^n|S_{21}|/d\omega^n = 0$) in the passband with no ripple. The insertion loss rolls off at $-20N\text{ dB/decade}$ ($-6N\text{ dB/octave}$). They are ideal for measurement equipment and audio/video signals requiring smooth passband gain.

📐 Chebyshev: Maximum Cutoff Sharpness

Chebyshev filters allow a small controlled ripple (e.g. 0.1 dB or 0.5 dB) in the passband to achieve significantly steeper transition into the stopband than Butterworth of the same order. Widely used in RF transmitter harmonic rejection and receiver preselectors where steep rolloff is paramount.

⏱️ Bessel-Thomson: Linear Phase & Constant Group Delay

Bessel filters optimize maximally flat group delay ($d\phi/d\omega = \text{const}$). Unlike Chebyshev filters which exhibit ringing and overshoot on pulse edges, Bessel filters preserve rectangular RF digital pulses, radar chirps, and high-speed modulation envelopes without distortion.

📡 High Frequency Microstrip Realization

At microwave frequencies above 1 GHz, lumped inductors and capacitors suffer from self-resonance. Richard's Transformation and Kuroda's Identities convert these lumped LC ladder values into commensurate distributed transmission line stubs (open and shorted microstrip lines).
